THE CHALLENGE

Kids spend 80% of their waking hours outside class. Most of them run on last-minute scrambles and whoever's available.

After school, half days and break weeks are where the skills that last get built. If you run a school or program, you own a share of those hours, and you're usually missing four things.

School takes up about 20% of a kid's waking hours. Source: Chris Gabrieli, Time to Learn, via Harvard Ed. Magazine.

THE DURABLE DOZEN

Jobs will change. These won't.

Durable skills are the human skills that last across every career. Each session builds one, so whatever path lights your kid up, they carry these with them.

  1. 01 LeadershipSteps up and brings others along.
  2. 02 CharacterDoes the right thing when no one is watching.
  3. 03 CollaborationBuilds something better together.
  4. 04 CommunicationSays it clearly. Listens fully.
  5. 05 CreativitySees options others miss.
  6. 06 Critical ThinkingAsks why and checks the facts.
  7. 07 MetacognitionKnows how they learn best.
  8. 08 MindfulnessStays calm and present under pressure.
  9. 09 Growth MindsetTreats mistakes as information.
  10. 10 FortitudeKeeps going when it gets hard.
  11. 11 EmpathyUnderstands what others feel.
  12. 12 DesignMakes it, tests it, makes it better.

Durable skills adapted from the America Succeeds framework. Each one sits alongside the Uncommon Core, our map of 30 life skills.

HOW TO READ A STAR

Every star is a path. Size is skill. Brightness is interest.

Every star is a career path your kid tried with a Catalyst. How big it is shows how strong they got. How bright it is shows how much it lit them up. Its points show which durable skills that path builds. The two don't always match, and that's the point.

Big and bright

Strong and loves it. A true strength. Build on it.

Small and bright

New at it, but lit up. A spark worth feeding.

Big and dim

Good at it, not excited by it. A tool, not a calling.

Small and dim

Tried it. Now they know. That's worth knowing too.

THE POINTS

Twelve points. One per durable skill.

Every star has the same twelve points in the same places. The longer the point, the more that path leans on the skill. A chef's star reaches toward Creativity and Design. A surgeon's reaches toward Mindfulness. Line up a kid's stars and you can see which skills run through everything they love.

THE CONSTELLATION REPORT

See which paths they're good at, and which lit them up.

At the end of a program, every family gets a Constellation Report. Every star is a career path their kid actually tried with a Catalyst. Size shows how strong they got. Brightness shows how much it lit them up. The points show the durable skills that path builds, always in the same spot, so you can see which skills keep showing up. Plus one North Star Goal for what's next.

WHY THE BAR IS HIGH

Not every parent is a Catalyst. One bad hour can close a door for good.

ONE GREAT HOUR

A nurse hands a nine-year-old a needle and a banana and talks her through her first stitch.

She goes home saying, "I could do that."
ONE BAD HOUR

An engineer who'd rather be anywhere else reads forty minutes of slides and snaps at the first question.

A kid decides engineering isn't for people like him. For good.

Kids don't separate the adult from the career. So we don't place people who are great at their job. We place people who are great at their job and great with kids. That's why every Catalyst is vetted, trained, observed and rated, and why some never make it into a room.
