Person by Person

We vet people, not employers.

Working at a great program doesn't make someone a Catalyst, and neither does having a great job. Kids don't separate the adult from the career, so one bad hour can close a door for good. Every Catalyst earns their place on their own.

NOT ENOUGH ON ITS OWN

Works for a well-known program, has an impressive title, or is a parent at the school.

WHAT MAKES A CATALYST

An interesting job, the real tools to show it, great with kids, able to teach, and able to find the moments in their work that light a kid up.

RelationshipsKids trust them fast. They learn names and pull in the kid hanging back.
EngagementKids' hands are busy, not just their eyes, and they ask for another round.
RigorKids learn the real skill at their size, and every kid can teach one thing back.

Four stars or better on all three, or no placement.

Who Kids See

We ask about representation. Out loud.

Most networks get quiet here. We don't. When a kid sees someone who shares their story doing work they never pictured, the door opens wider. So you can tell us what matters for your room: gender, race and ethnicity, language, first-generation college, disability, or a path like an HBCU, a trade or the military.

Two things we ask in return.

  • Tell us why it matters for your kids or your event. "Our girls have never met a woman engineer" helps us plan. A checkbox alone doesn't.
  • Stay open. Kids get lifted by people who don't share their identity all the time. A girl can find her calling through a man who's great at his craft. A kid can catch fire watching someone from a world nothing like theirs. The best room has both.

Representation is something we plan for, never a gate. Every Catalyst clears the same bar, and no one is turned away for who they are.
